Is Nextdoor Worth It for Contractors?

AP By Aaron Phillips · Booked Job · Updated June 2026
Short answer: Nextdoor's free side — neighbors recommending you by name — is one of the better lead sources a local shop can have, and it costs nothing. The paid side is a different product entirely. Judge it the same way you judge everything else: cost per booked job.

When a neighbor asks "who do you use for plumbing?" and three people type your name, that's the warmest lead in local marketing: exclusive, pre-vouched, and zero cost per lead.

The free side is the good side

Neighbor recommendations are warm, exclusive, and free.

When a neighbor asks "who do you use for plumbing?" and three people type your name, that's the warmest lead in local marketing: exclusive, pre-vouched, and zero cost per lead. Claim your business page, keep it accurate, and ask happy customers to recommend you there the same way you ask for a Google review.

The paid side has to earn its place

Judge it on cost per booked job, not impressions.

Every channel gets the same test in this shop: what does a booked job cost? A booked job runs ~$542 via Angi versus ~$168 via Google Local Service Ads once close rates are counted. If Nextdoor ads land under your Google LSA number, keep spending. If they don't, that money belongs in LSA or reviews. That's the whole decision. Do the math: cost per booked job calculator.

It won't replace Google — it stacks on it

Nextdoor is a referral amplifier, not a search engine.

People go to Nextdoor to ask their neighbors and to Google to find a pro right now. You need the second one working regardless: 91% of homeowners won't consider a shop under 4 stars (BrightLocal 2026), and the map pack is still where the urgent, ready-to-book calls come from. Treat Nextdoor as extra referral surface, not a substitute.

Answer Nextdoor leads like they're on fire

Speed decides who gets the recommendation thread.

Recommendation threads move in hours. 78% of homeowners hire whoever responds first (Lead Connect 2026), and replying in 5 minutes instead of 30 makes you up to 100x more likely to connect (MIT Sloan 2026). A neighborhood thread you answer on Thursday for a Tuesday post is a thread someone else already won.

How to test it in 60 days

Small budget, tracked number, one honest decision.

Run a small monthly budget, use a distinct tracking number or a "how'd you hear about us?" field, and count booked jobs, not leads. At 60 days, divide spend by jobs. Under your LSA cost — scale it. Over — kill it without sentiment. That's the same test that saves contractors from every shiny channel: evaluate any marketing with simple math.

Frequently asked questions

Is Nextdoor worth it for contractors?

The free business page and neighbor recommendations are worth the setup time for almost every local shop — exclusive, warm, no per-lead cost. Paid Nextdoor ads are only worth it if they book jobs for less than your best channel, which for most contractors is Google LSA at ~$168 per booked job vs ~$542 via Angi.

Are Nextdoor leads better than Angi leads?

A neighbor recommendation is usually far better than a shared platform lead, because it's exclusive and comes with a vouch. Shared leads get sold to as many as 12 pros, which is why a booked job through Angi averages ~$542 vs ~$168 on Google LSA.

How do I get recommended on Nextdoor?

Claim your page, keep hours and services accurate, and ask satisfied customers to recommend you there — the same habit that builds Google reviews. Then answer every thread fast; 78% of homeowners hire whoever responds first (Lead Connect 2026).
