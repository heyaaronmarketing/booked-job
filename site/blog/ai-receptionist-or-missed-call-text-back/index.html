An AI receptionist costs about ten times what a missed-call text-back does, and for most small shops it doesn't win you more jobs.

You missed a call while you were under a sink. A salesperson has a pitch: an AI receptionist that answers every call, 24/7, in a friendly voice. It runs about $300 a month. That's $3,600 a year. Before you sign anything, do the arithmetic.

The cheap alternative is missed-call text-back. A missed call fires an automatic text to the caller: "Sorry we missed you, this is Dave at Dave's Plumbing, what do you need?" That's often about $30 a month. Same job of not dropping the lead. One-tenth the price. The question isn't which is fancier. It's which one actually books the job.

What a lead actually wants: a fast answer

Leads go to whoever responds first, and a text in seconds beats a bot that fumbles a conversation.

Here's the number that should drive every decision you make about your phone: responding in 5 minutes instead of 30 makes you up to 100x more likely to connect with a lead, and 21x more likely to qualify it (Lead Response Management study, Prof. James Oldroyd). Speed is the whole game. A homeowner with a burst pipe calls three plumbers. The first human to call back usually wins.

A missed-call text-back hits that window instantly. The caller gets a real text with your name, and they can just reply. You see it and call back. We walk through the setup in missed-call text-back for contractors and why speed to lead beats almost everything else you could spend money on.

Where the $300 bot earns its keep (and where it doesn't)

An AI receptionist only pays off at high call volume you genuinely can't staff — most small shops aren't there.

Be fair to the expensive option. If you're a multi-truck shop drowning in calls and you're already losing real jobs to voicemail at noon, a receptionist that answers and books can pencil out. At enough volume, $3,600 a year is cheap next to the jobs you'd otherwise lose.

But most shops aren't there. If you miss a handful of calls a day, you don't need a machine holding full conversations with your customers. You need the caller to know you saw them and to get a callback fast. The bot adds cost, a new failure point, and a layer between you and the person about to hand you money. The verdict for most shops

Start with the ~$30 text-back; only reach for a receptionist when call volume proves you need it.

Here's the honest call: for most one-to-three-truck shops, spend the $30, not the $300. Get missed-call text-back running this week. It closes the gap that's actually costing you jobs — the silent dropped call — without putting a robot in front of your customers.

Revisit the AI receptionist only when your own call logs prove you're losing jobs you can't catch any other way. If you want a human to look at your numbers and tell you straight, talk to a person. Frequently asked questions

How much does an AI receptionist cost for a contractor?

A typical AI receptionist runs about $300 a month, which is roughly $3,600 a year. Missed-call text-back does the core job of not dropping a lead for about $30 a month. For most small shops the cheaper tool wins.

Does missed-call text-back actually work?

Yes, because it hits the lead fast. Responding in 5 minutes instead of 30 makes you up to 100x more likely to connect with a lead (Lead Response Management study, Prof. James Oldroyd). A text that goes out the second you miss a call keeps you in that window.

When is an AI receptionist actually worth it?

When you have enough call volume that you're losing real jobs to voicemail and can't staff a human to answer. At that point $3,600 a year can pencil out. Below that, you're paying for a robot you don't need.
