How to Respond to a Bad Review

AP By Aaron Phillips · Booked Job · Updated June 2026
Short answer: The reply isn't for the person who left the review. It's for the hundred homeowners who'll read it while deciding whether to call you. Short, calm, specific, and offline — that's the whole formula, and it turns a 1-star into proof you're a pro.

You will almost never change the reviewer's mind. You will absolutely change what the next reader thinks. 91% of homeowners won't consider a shop under 4 stars (BrightLocal 2026) — but a calm, specific reply under a bad review reads as competence, and buyers notice.

Treat every reply as a two-sentence ad for how you handle problems.

The formula: acknowledge, correct the record, take it offline

Three short moves, no argument.

Acknowledge the frustration in one line. Add one factual detail that quietly corrects the record (the date, the scope, the warranty you offered). Then give a name and a number and move it off the platform. Three sentences. Never four paragraphs — length reads as guilt.

Templates you can paste today

Copy, swap the details, post within 24 hours.

Wrong expectation: "Thanks for the feedback, Dana — I'm sorry the timeline felt long. Our quote covered the coil replacement only, and the parts shipped on the 14th. I'd still like to make this right: call me directly at 555-0147. — Mike, owner."

Not our customer: "We take every review seriously, but we don't have a job on record at this address. If we worked with you under another name, please call me at 555-0147 so I can look into it. — Mike, owner."

We genuinely blew it: "You're right, and I'm sorry. We missed the window and didn't call ahead. I've refunded the trip charge and we've changed how we confirm appointments. If you'll give us another shot, ask for me. — Mike, owner."

What never to say

No arguing, no legal threats, no customer details.

Don't relitigate the invoice line by line. Don't mention lawyers. Don't post the customer's address, health details, or payment history. And don't ask for the review to be taken down in public — that reads as a shop that argues, which is exactly what the next homeowner is scanning for.

The real fix is volume

Bury a bad review under honest new ones.

One 1-star out of 300 is a rounding error. One out of nine is your identity. The shops winning the map pack carry about 519 reviews (HVAC) and about 337 (plumbers) — at that volume a bad review costs you almost nothing. Build the habit: the one-tap review system and how to get more Google reviews.

Frequently asked questions

Should I respond to every bad review?

Yes, within about 24 hours, in three sentences: acknowledge, add one factual detail, and move it offline with a name and number. The reply is written for the next reader, not the reviewer.

Can I get a bad Google review removed?

Only if it violates Google's policies (spam, off-topic, personal attacks, a competitor). You can flag it, but don't count on removal and never demand it publicly — respond calmly instead and out-volume it with real reviews.

How much does one bad review actually hurt?

It depends entirely on volume. 91% of homeowners won't consider a shop under 4 stars (BrightLocal 2026), so one 1-star against a handful of reviews is serious — against a few hundred it barely moves your rating.
