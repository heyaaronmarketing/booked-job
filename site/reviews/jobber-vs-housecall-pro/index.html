Jobber vs Housecall Pro: Which One, and Why

Two good tools, two different shops. Jobber is cheaper, cleaner, and easier; Housecall Pro does more — financing, instant payouts, proposals — for more money. Pick by the crew you actually run, not the feature list you'll never touch.

Verdict —<br>Depends on your crew

Jobber for most small shops — cheaper at every tier, easier to use, better customer-facing CRM, higher user-satisfaction scores. Housecall Pro for multi-tech operations that will actually use financing, instant payouts, and proposals. If you'd be buying Housecall's breadth to use a third of it, buy Jobber and keep the difference.

Finding 01

The one-line answer

If you're a solo op or small crew that mostly needs to quote, schedule, invoice, and get paid, go Jobber — it's cheaper, simpler, and crews actually adopt it. If you run several techs and sell bigger jobs where consumer financing, instant payouts, and slick proposals move real money, Housecall Pro earns its higher price. Everything below is just the receipts for that.

Questions, answered straight

Is Jobber or Housecall Pro better for a small contractor?
For most small crews, Jobber — it's cheaper at every tier, easier to use, has a better customer-facing CRM, and scores higher on user satisfaction. Housecall Pro is the better pick for multi-tech shops that will actually use its financing, instant payouts, and proposal tools.
Which is cheaper, Jobber or Housecall Pro?
Jobber, at list price, at every tier. Jobber Core starts around $29–$49/mo vs Housecall Pro Basic near $79/mo, and the gap holds as you move up. Housecall Pro's total cost also climbs faster once you add seats, add-ons, and card processing.
What do people complain about?
Jobber's most-cited gripe is finicky QuickBooks sync and some slowdown on large, complex setups. Housecall Pro's are a weaker Android app than iOS, inconsistent support, and aggressive upsell calls. Both are well-rated overall.
