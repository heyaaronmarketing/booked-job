Rosie: The $49 AI That Answers (and Not Much More)

Rosie is the cheap-seats AI receptionist — $49 to answer your phone with a warm-sounding voice. Fair price, real limits: booking doesn't turn on until you pay triple, it's voice-only, and you bring your own phone system. Here's who it actually fits.

Verdict —<br>Cheapest honest entry

Fine for simple answering. At $49/mo Rosie is the lowest-risk way to put a decent-sounding AI on your line — if all you need is “take a message, give hours.” But appointment booking only kicks in at the $149 Scale tier, it's voice-only (no web chat), and you supply your own phone number. For a shop that mostly needs messages captured, it's honest value. For real booking, price the Scale tier, not the headline.

Finding 01

What $49 actually buys

Rosie answers your phone 24/7 with a voice reviewers consistently call warmer than most competitors, takes messages, and texts you the details — starting at $49/mo for 250 minutes. There's a free app on iOS and Android. It's a genuinely low entry point, and it doesn't pretend to be an enterprise platform. The honest catch is what's not in the $49 plan.

Questions, answered straight

Is Rosie AI worth $49 a month?
For a shop that just needs its phone answered and messages captured, yes — it's the cheapest honest entry and the voice is warm. But the $49 Starter tier doesn't include appointment booking (that's the $149 Scale tier), it's voice-only, and you bring your own phone number. Budget the Scale tier if you actually want jobs booked.
Does Rosie book appointments?
Only on the Scale plan ($149/mo) and up. The $49 Starter plan is answering and messages only.
Rosie vs Goodcall?
Rosie tends to give more minutes per dollar and a warmer voice, which suits most small service businesses. Goodcall's per-unique-caller pricing can be better for long or repeat calls. Both are entry-level; neither replaces fixing your own fast call response.
