Thumbtack for Pros: Worth It, or Death by a Thousand $30 Charges?

Thumbtack is the least-rotten of the lead apps &mdash; no membership fee, you set your own budget, and you can get credited for some junk. But you still pay per lead, not per job, and the charges stack up fast if you can't answer in seconds. Here's exactly who it works for.

Verdict —<br>Only if you work it

Conditional. Thumbtack is more honest than Angi — no membership, you control the budget, refunds exist for some bad contacts. But it still charges you per lead whether or not it becomes a job, so it only pays off if you answer instantly, pick narrow categories, and track your close rate. If you're slow to the phone or in a saturated trade, it bleeds you.

Finding 01

How it actually charges you

Thumbtack charges per lead — when a homeowner contacts you (or you reach out), you pay, whether or not it ever becomes a job. There's no membership or annual fee, and you set your own weekly budget, which is genuinely better than Angi's model. But the per-lead price swings hard by trade and market. Contractors report 2026 ranges around $15–$35 for cleaning, $20–$45 handyman, $30–$70 painting, $45–$120 plumbing, and $50–$200+ for HVAC and roofing. At those numbers, two dead leads can cost more than the job you're chasing.

The part nobody prices

Watch it
“Only pay for leads that are a match.”
A “match” means the homeowner's request fits your settings — not that they'll answer, hire, or were even serious. Pros report being charged for contacts that never replied. Thumbtack does credit some bad leads, but you have to catch them and request it. Budget for a chunk of every dollar going to leads that go nowhere.

Finding 04

If you run Thumbtack, run it like this

It's not a scam like some of the others — it's a tool that punishes slow, broad, and untracked. So:

  1. Answer every lead in under 5 minutes. Thumbtack rewards fast responders with better visibility, and speed is what closes them anyway.
  2. Narrow your categories and service area hard. Broad settings = more junk leads you still pay for.
  3. Request credit on every lead that was clearly bad (never a real request, wrong service, outside your area). It's not automatic.
  4. Track close rate per source every month. The day Thumbtack's cost-per-job beats your own channels, cap it.

Questions, answered straight

Is Thumbtack worth it for contractors in 2026?
It can be, conditionally. Unlike Angi there's no membership fee and you set your own budget, but you're charged per lead whether or not it converts. It pays off mainly for pros who answer within minutes, pick narrow categories, and track close rate. Slow responders or those in saturated trades usually lose money.
Does Thumbtack charge you for leads that don't respond?
Yes — you're charged when a lead comes in, not when it converts. Thumbtack will credit some clearly-bad leads, but you have to request it; it isn't automatic.
Is Thumbtack better than Angi?
For most pros, yes — marginally. No membership fee, you control the budget, and refunds for junk leads are easier to get. But both charge per lead rather than per job, so the honest move is to fix your own lead response first and treat either app as a capped experiment.
