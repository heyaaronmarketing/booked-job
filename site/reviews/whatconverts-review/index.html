WhatConverts: Which Ad Actually Made the Phone Ring

Call + form tracking that tags every lead with the ad that earned it. The tool that turns “I spend $2k on marketing” into “this channel costs me $140 a job, that one costs $900.”

Verdict —<br>Buy it, once you're paying for leads

Buy it the moment you're spending on more than one channel. WhatConverts answers the one question that decides your whole budget: which ad, which keyword, which channel actually made the phone ring? If you run Google Ads and LSA and organic and can't tell which is working, you're flying blind — and this fixes that for the price of a few leads. If you run one channel or mostly referrals, it's overkill. Just ask “how'd you hear about us?”

Here's the trap every contractor who starts spending falls into: you run Google Ads, you sign up for Local Service Ads, you've got some organic traffic and a yard sign out front. A call comes in, you book the job. Which one of those earned it? You have no idea. So you keep paying for all of them — including the ones doing nothing.

WhatConverts is call tracking + form tracking. It gives you tracking phone numbers and tags every call, form, and chat with the exact source that drove it — down to the campaign, ad, and keyword. The point isn't the dashboard. It's that it turns a guess into a number: cost per booked job, by channel. That's the only number that actually tells you where to put your next dollar.

The thing to understand before you buy

The $30 entry price is real, but it's not the whole story.

Read the fine print
&ldquo;Track every lead for thirty bucks a month.&rdquo;
The base tier covers basic call/form tracking, but tracking numbers bill on top (~$5–$20 each), and the multi-channel attribution that makes this worth owning sits on the Plus tier and up. Budget for the plan that actually answers your question, not the cheapest one.

Still cheap next to what it saves: if it shows you a channel burning $900 a booked job, killing that one channel pays for the tool for a year.

When it's worth it, when it's overkill

Straight version:

  1. Worth it when you're spending real money on two or more channels (Ads + LSA + SEO) and genuinely can't tell which one is booking jobs. That's exactly when the wrong guess costs you thousands.
  2. Overkill when you run one channel, or most of your work is referrals and repeat customers. You don't need a dashboard to know where those came from.
  3. What it actually gets you: cost per booked job by channel — not cost per lead, not clicks. Tie the booked revenue back to the source and you can finally cut the dead channel instead of the whole budget.
  4. The honest catch: a tool only measures the leak; it doesn't fix it. If the problem is that leads ring and nobody answers, tracking just proves you're losing them. Fix the answering first (that's us), then measure what's left.

Questions, answered straight

Is WhatConverts worth it for contractors?
If you're spending on two or more marketing channels and can't tell which is actually booking jobs, yes — it pays for itself the first time it exposes a channel that's wasting money. If you run one channel or mostly referrals, it's overkill; just ask callers how they found you.
How much does WhatConverts cost?
Published plans run roughly $30/mo (Call Tracking) up to $160/mo (Elite), with a 14-day trial and a $30 usage credit, and no free plan. Tracking phone numbers bill on top at about $5–$20 per number per month, so budget for the plan plus your numbers. Confirm current tiers on their pricing page.
WhatConverts vs CallRail?
Both do call and form tracking with lead attribution; they're close competitors and pricing is similar. WhatConverts leans hard into lead-level reporting and value tracking (tying revenue to source), which is exactly what you want to compute cost per booked job by channel. For most contractors either works — pick on price and whichever integrates with the ad platforms you actually run.
