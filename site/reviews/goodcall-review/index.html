Goodcall: Easy to Build, Priced by the Caller

Goodcall's pitch is simplicity — a drag-and-drop call-flow builder a non-techie can actually use, at $79/mo. The fine print: it's priced per unique caller (not per minute), you can't port your number, and there's no SMS. Here's who that math works for.

Verdict —<br>Easy, with real limits

Decent if your calls are long or repeat. Goodcall is the easiest of these to set up yourself, and per-unique-caller billing with unlimited minutes is a genuine win when callers talk a while or call back often. But no number porting (you use their number), no SMS, and limited voices are real constraints, and its user rating runs lower than Rosie's. Good for simple, long-call lines; not a full front desk.

Finding 01

Where it's genuinely good

Two real strengths. First, the drag-and-drop call-flow builder is usable by a non-technical owner — you can map out “press 1 for a quote” without a developer. Second, pricing is per unique caller with unlimited minutes ($79/mo, ~100 unique callers on Starter), so a long or repeat call doesn't cost extra. For a shop whose calls run long, that model beats per-minute billing.

Questions, answered straight

How much does Goodcall cost?
Starter is about $79/mo for roughly 100 unique callers with unlimited minutes; extra unique callers run ~$0.50–$0.79 each. It's priced per unique caller rather than per minute, which favors long or repeat calls. Multi-location setups get pricier because pricing is also per agent.
What are Goodcall's biggest limitations?
Three come up repeatedly: no number porting (you must use a Goodcall number), no SMS (it can't text a lead back), and limited voice options. For many service businesses, not being able to keep your existing number or text customers is a dealbreaker.
Goodcall or Rosie?
Goodcall is easier to build and its per-caller/unlimited-minutes model suits long or repeat calls. Rosie is cheaper to start, has a warmer voice, and gives more minutes per dollar for simple answering. Neither replaces fast human call response for a small shop.
