Housecall Pro: More Tool Than Most Shops Need

Housecall Pro does more than Jobber — built-in consumer financing, instant payouts, slick proposals — and charges more for it. Whether that's worth it comes down to one question: do you have techs to dispatch and jobs big enough to finance, or are you a two-truck shop paying for features you'll never open?

Verdict —<br>Buy it for breadth

Conditional yes. If you run multiple techs and want financing, instant payouts, and strong proposals in one place, Housecall Pro earns its price. If you're a small crew that mostly needs quote-schedule-invoice, Jobber does it for less. Watch the add-ons and the ~2.59% card processing — the headline plan isn't the real bill.

Finding 01

Where it beats the cheaper option

Housecall Pro is a full field-service platform — scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, CRM — with extras most competitors don't bundle: consumer financing you can offer at the door, instant payouts (Instapay), expense cards, and a stronger proposal / upsell toolkit. For a shop running several techs and selling bigger-ticket work, those tools move money. It's a real, well-built product with high ratings — 4.7/5 on Capterra across thousands of reviews.

The honest knocks

Fair warning
“Everything your business needs in one app.”
Mostly true — but the recurring complaints are real: the Android app lags the iOS one (a years-long gripe), support response can be slow, and the sales outreach is aggressive. And the all-in cost (seats + add-ons + ~2.59% processing) runs well above the sticker. Great tool; just don't buy the Max plan to use a third of it.

Questions, answered straight

Is Housecall Pro worth it in 2026?
For shops running multiple techs that want built-in financing, instant payouts, and strong proposals, yes — it earns its higher price. For a solo operator or very small crew that mainly needs quoting, scheduling, and invoicing, Jobber usually delivers the same core for less.
How much does Housecall Pro really cost?
List plans run roughly $79 (Basic, 1 user), $189 (Essentials, up to 5), and $329 (Max, up to 8) month-to-month, lower annually. The real bill is higher once you add seats, add-ons, and card processing (~2.59%) — budget $150–$350/mo all-in for a typical small shop.
What are the biggest Housecall Pro complaints?
The most consistent ones are a weaker Android app than iOS, inconsistent support response times, and aggressive sales/upsell outreach. The core product is well-rated (4.7/5 on Capterra), but those three come up repeatedly.
